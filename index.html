The Tollway Concession Analogy

Government Builds Passive Civil Assets, OpCo Manages Active Network

  • Public Civil Works Investment (CapEx): Like public expressways (e.g. SCTEX), the government finances civil trenching, manholes, shelters, and multi-duct pipes as permanent public capital assets.
  • Private DTIP Concessionaire (OpCo): Deploys active electronics (ODFs, DWDM/OTN routers), operates the NOC, maintains 99.9% SLAs, and monetizes via carrier-neutral wheeling tariffs.
  • The Dig-Once Economic Driver: Civil excavation accounts for >50% to 70% of deployment costs. Synchronizing with DPWH and LGU road works slashes deployment costs to less than one-third.
📜

The DTIP Regulatory Catalyst (RA 12234)

Konektadong Pinoy Act & Mandatory Open-Access Mandates

  • No Congressional Franchise Bottleneck: Classifies operators as Data Transmission Industry Participants (DTIP), shifting market entry to streamlined administrative NTC registration.
  • Mandatory FRAND Open-Access: Legally prohibits anti-competitive hoarding of passive infrastructure. Requires access on Fair, Reasonable, and Non-Discriminatory terms.
  • Hyper-Local Retail Ecosystem: Local micro-ISPs connect into standardized local aggregation POPs via transparent wheeling tariffs, driving retail sachet vouchers & micro-broadband into underserved barangays.

Direct 48V DC Solar-Battery Architecture & Civil Standards

Insulating Critical Telecom Nodes from Utility Surges & Typhoons

☀️ Direct 48V DC Solar-Battery Bus

Direct 48V DC bus connection completely isolates telecom node electronics from alternating-current grid voltage surges, eliminating failure-prone inverters and ensuring 100% uptime during provincial blackouts.

🚇 4-Way 14/10mm Microduct Bundle

Encased in sand bedding and lean concrete at 0.8m–1.2m depth with magnetic warning tape. Sidewalk micro-trenching utilizes 2-way 12/9mm microducts cut along curb lines for rapid, low-impact deployment.

📦 Pre-Cast Polymer Handholes

Positioned every 300m–500m along utility corridors for rapid mid-span fiber blowing and tenant interconnects without pavement breaking.

The Southwestern Coastal Gap: Bagac to Mariveles

Current Status: Open-Ended Spur Vulnerability

The November 2024 regional backbone dataset explicitly tags BAGAC (2) - LOWER (30.13 km) and MARIVELES (13.15 km) under ROUTE > NOT IMPLEMENTED.

Because this link is missing, fiber lines going into Morong and Bagac terminate as an unredundant spur. If a fallen tree or backhoe damages the trans-peninsular aerial span crossing Mount Samat (Pilar to Bagac), the entire western municipality cluster is completely severed.

BatFIRST Resiliency Mandate: Construct a 36.8 km coastal underground microduct conduit along Gov. Joaquin J. Linao Road from Bagac Town Proper to the Mariveles Freeport Zone. This transforms the network into a 360° closed-loop, self-healing municipal ring that reroutes traffic in sub-seconds.
🛣️

1. Highway Bypasses & New Alignments

Roman Superhighway, Hermosa-Morong Economic Access Road

The ₱4.27 Billion Hermosa-Morong Economic Access Road (19 km) is currently under active construction. Implementing mandatory Dig-Once multi-ducts directly into this road project provides a low-cost, shallow-trench cross-peninsular conduit connecting Hermosa and Subic to Morong before pavement is completed.

🌊

2. River Flood-Control Retaining Walls

DPWH and Provincial Concrete River Dikes

Historic town centers in Bataan (Orani, Samal, Abucay, Balanga) are centered around river mouths. Anchoring galvanized steel or UPVC conduit brackets directly to concrete retaining wall copings above historical high-water marks eliminates the need to break downtown paved roads.

🌾

3. Irrigation Canal Dikes (NIA)

National Irrigation Administration Service Roads

Unpaved canal service roads and dikes form soft-dig, obstacle-free linear paths that connect agricultural interior barangays directly to municipal POP aggregation nodes without encountering highway right-of-way fees.

⚡

4. High-Voltage Transmission Easements

NGCP & TRANSCO Ground Rights-of-Way

Transmission corridors route power from Bataan's massive energy plants directly north through Central Luzon, offering continuous linear paths insulated from municipal traffic interference and road-widening disruptions.
